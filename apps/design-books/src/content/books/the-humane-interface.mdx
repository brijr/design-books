## Why it matters

Raskin asks interface designers to judge conventions by cognitive fit rather than familiarity alone. The book's strong proposals remain valuable because they expose how easily tools demand attention that belongs to the user's task.

## Best for

Best for interaction designers, engineers, and product thinkers interested in first principles, modes, habits, and the cost of interface complexity.

## What you'll learn

You will learn to analyze interaction through attention, locus of control, habituation, monotony, and information efficiency—and to imagine alternatives to inherited patterns.
