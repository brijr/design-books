## Why it matters

Pullin challenges the assumption that assistive products must look clinical or that inclusive design should disappear into a universal solution. He treats disability as a source of design possibility, identity, and richer debate.

## Best for

Best for product designers, accessibility practitioners, and anyone reconsidering the relationship between specialized needs and mainstream aesthetics.

## What you'll learn

You will learn to question stigma, invisibility, fashion, personalization, and the productive role of difference in the design of objects and interactions.
