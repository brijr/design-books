## Origins

Russia, war then revolution. Painters put down the easel and picked up the poster, the kiosk, the book cover. The claim is not decoration. It is construction — graphics as a machine that produces a new public.

Lissitzky's wedge is the field mark: a triangle that means something. Rodchenko's camera looks down from a new angle because the old eye is bourgeois. Stepanova puts the same geometry on cloth.

The state later wanted a different picture. By the mid-1930s Socialist Realism replaces the bar and the wedge with the heroic body. The graphic survives as an export — into the Bauhaus, into Tschichold, into every poster that leans.

## In the wild

Festival posters, "revolutionary" tech decks, any campaign that wants urgency without a product shot. The cheap version is a red bar and a sans.

The real thing has force. The page is going somewhere. If it is only stylish, the construction has already failed.
