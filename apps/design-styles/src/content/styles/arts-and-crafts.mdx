## Origins

Britain, late nineteenth century. The factory could print anything and make it look like nothing. Morris, Ruskin, and the workshops around them answered with the handmade book, the honest chair, the guild.

It is a moral movement that left a visual one. The page is dense, the ornament is flat, the type has a medieval body. Nature appears as pattern, not as landscape.

The Bauhaus later kept the workshop and dropped the medieval costume. That is the through-line: making as the point, not the look of making.

## In the wild

Letterpress posters, craft-beer labels, and any brand that wants "handmade" without showing a workshop. The revival is constant because the complaint is constant: industry still makes cheap copies.

A true citation shows the tool. A fake one adds a kraft texture in Photoshop.
