# Prior art across the family's three spaces

Type: research
Status: resolved

## Question

What already exists in each of the three spaces, what does it do well, and
where are the gaps design-sites can own? Three sweeps:

1. **Style/movement references** (the See space): Wikipedia's movement
   articles, design-history timelines and courses, museum collection sites,
   visual style catalogs and UI-trend galleries.
2. **Designer reference/tool sites** (the Use space): type-scale generators,
   contrast checkers, size/spec references, cheat-sheet sites, and whether
   anything unifies answer-first references with embedded tools.
3. **Philosophy/idea collections** (the Think space): principles.design,
   lawsofux.com, design-manifesto collections, academic overviews.

For each sweep: the 3–6 strongest incumbents, what each does well, what's
missing (curation? design quality? citations? interconnection?), and the
positioning opportunity for our site given its verb and the family-vision
model. Note anything worth linking to rather than competing with.

Findings land on branch `research/prior-art` as a markdown file; link it here
on resolution.

## Answer

Full survey (incumbent-by-incumbent, visited August 2026): branch
`research/prior-art`, file `docs/research/prior-art.md`.

**SEE (design-styles.com).** Strongest incumbent: Wikipedia's movement
articles — deep, well-cited, and visually starved (3 images on the
International Typographic Style article). Museums (V&A, Cooper Hewitt, Google
Arts & Culture) have the imagery but no field-guide narrative; Awwwards owns
the current moment with no historical spine; trend galleries decay
(brutalistwebsites stale since ~2018, godly.website dead) and the classic
indie incumbent Design Is History is now a dead domain. Positioning: own
**recognition** — a timeline-spined visual field guide with per-style visual
anatomy, connecting historical movements to living UI trends, citing museum
collections rather than competing with them.

**USE (design-reference.com).** Strongest incumbents: WebAIM's contrast
checker (authoritative, dated, single-purpose) and typescale.com (polished
tool, zero reference layer); iosref.com and Practical Typography each prove
half the model (maintained specs + small tools; answer-first cited prose).
The space is structurally fragmented — one tool per site, one topic per site,
plus ad-wrapped cheat-sheet farms — and **nothing unifies answer-first
reference + embedded tools + cited primary sources. There is no designer-side
MDN.** Positioning: own the unified reference desk — answer on top, tool
embedded, primary source (HIG/WCAG/Material) cited underneath;
server-rendered (several incumbents are JS shells invisible to
crawlers/agents).

**THINK (design-philosophies.com).** Strongest incumbent: lawsofux.com —
beautiful, maintained, but UX-psychology heuristics only, presented as
settled fact. principles.design and designprinciplesftw collect team
principles, not philosophies; designmanifestos.org is the primary-text
archive (link, don't rehost); IxDF has authority buried in an SEO membership
funnel; worst SERP pollution of the three spaces (A' Design Award content
farm ranks for "design philosophy"). **Nobody maps schools of thought and
nobody steelmans.** Positioning: own the annotated atlas — strongest form
first, position/critique second, cross-linked to SEE's movements, USE's
practices, and READ's books.

Cross-cutting: the best incumbents everywhere are single-maintainer labors of
love that eventually stall or die — maintenance is a differentiator — and
every incumbent is an island; the four-verb interconnection is the moat.
